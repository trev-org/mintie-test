---
title: "Identity change management"
description: "Plan, approve, test, and roll back high-impact identity changes"
groups: ["enterprise"]
---

Use a controlled change process for updates that can affect organization-wide
sign-in or provisioning. Examples include certificate rotation, redirect URI
changes, claim mappings, group assignments, conditional-access policies, and
SCIM scope changes.

## Before the change

- Assign a change owner, technical reviewer, and rollback owner.
- Describe the expected user impact and affected identity flows.
- Record the last known successful SSO and provisioning tests.
- Preserve an active administrator session for recovery.
- Confirm that the rollback procedure and required credentials are available.
- Schedule a validation window with the identity-provider owner.

<Tip>
  Change one identity control at a time. Combining certificate, claim, and
  assignment changes makes failures harder to isolate and roll back.
</Tip>

## Execute safely

<Steps>
  <Step title="Capture the starting state">
    Record non-secret configuration values, connection identifiers, ownership,
    and test results. Never copy credentials into a change ticket.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply the smallest change">
    Make only the approved update and note the exact completion time for
    correlation with provider events.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Validate a pilot user">
    Test an assigned standard user before broadening the change. Confirm the
    expected identity, claims, group membership, and session behavior.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Validate the lifecycle">
    When provisioning is affected, test one create, update, and deactivate
    cycle with a dedicated account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitor and close">
    Watch provider failures and support volume through the validation window.
    Record results, exceptions, and follow-up owners.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Rollback triggers

Roll back when:

- administrator or standard-user sign-in fails unexpectedly,
- users receive incorrect roles or organization access,
- provisioning changes affect users outside the approved scope,
- failure rates exceed the agreed threshold, or
- validation cannot be completed within the change window.

For a broad outage, stop the change and follow the
[identity incident-response runbook](/operations/incident-response).
