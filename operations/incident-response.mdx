---
title: "Identity incident response"
description: "Contain an SSO or provisioning outage and restore access safely"
---

Use this runbook when SSO fails broadly, users are provisioned incorrectly, deactivated users retain access, or identity credentials may be exposed.

## First 15 minutes

<Steps>
  <Step title="Preserve access">
    Keep existing administrator sessions open. Do not sign out, disconnect the
    provider, or revoke credentials until you understand the blast radius.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Declare and assign">
    Name an incident lead, an IdP owner, a Mintie owner, and a communications
    owner. Use one channel and one timeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Determine scope">
    Identify the first failure time and whether the incident affects one user,
    one group, all users, SSO, SCIM, API access, or several systems.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop unrelated changes">
    Pause configuration edits and bulk provisioning. Preserve provider events,
    timestamps, and connection IDs.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Choose containment

| Incident | Initial containment |
| --- | --- |
| IdP service outage | Keep active sessions open and monitor provider status |
| Bad claim or app assignment | Roll back the specific IdP change |
| Expired certificate or secret | Follow the [credential rotation](/operations/rotate-credentials) runbook |
| Incorrect SCIM updates | Pause provisioning in the IdP before correcting records |
| Suspected credential exposure | Replace the credential, then revoke the exposed value |
| SSO locks out the organization | Use your approved emergency-access procedure |

<Warning>
  Disconnecting the provider rejects new SSO sign-ins immediately. Disabling
  **Require SSO** restores password sign-in, but only helps users who still
  have a usable password. Treat either action as a deliberate mitigation.
</Warning>

## Emergency-access preparation

Because SSO enforcement is organization-wide, establish the recovery process before an incident:

- Maintain at least two organization administrators.
- Store recovery instructions in an approved, access-controlled system.
- Define who may temporarily disable **Require SSO**.
- Test the procedure during a scheduled exercise.
- Review it whenever administrators or IdP ownership changes.

## Support bundle

Collect:

- Mintie organization and connection ID
- Incident start time, time zone, and last known successful sign-in
- Affected users and estimated scope
- IdP name, protocol, provider event IDs, and service-status result
- Recent changes to metadata, claims, assignments, policies, or credentials
- Redacted screenshots or response bodies

Never include passwords, tokens, client secrets, session cookies, private keys, or complete SAML assertions.

## Recovery and follow-up

1. Validate an administrator and a standard-user sign-in.
2. If SCIM was affected, run one create, update, and deactivate cycle.
3. Resume paused assignments or provisioning gradually.
4. Monitor failures and support volume until they return to baseline.
5. Document the cause, impact, remediation, and a prevention owner.
